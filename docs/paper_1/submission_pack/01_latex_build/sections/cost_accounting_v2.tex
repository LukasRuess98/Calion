\subsection{Cost accounting: economic cost versus the solver objective}
\label{subsec:costacct}

Every bias and regret figure in this paper is reported on the \emph{economic} operating
cost, not on the raw solver objective, and the two differ systematically. On Memmingen the
objective exceeds the economic cost by $38.7$--$41.0$\,\% depending on the level
(Table~S2, Supplementary Material). Because that difference is large enough to change the
apparent size of every comparison, this section states what it is and why the economic cost is the
right quantity.

Two mechanisms account for it, and neither is a distortion of the dispatch. First, carbon
enters the objective on a \emph{gross} basis (every tonne from fuel combustion and grid
import priced at the carbon price), whereas the reported cost applies the standard
cogeneration self-use allocation, crediting back the emissions of combined-heat-and-power
electricity that is exported rather than self-consumed. This is the avoided-emissions
convention conventional in district-heating studies, and on this network it amounts to
$55$--$58$\,kEUR. Second, the objective carries a thermal-storage cycling penalty of
$2$\,EUR per MWh of throughput, worth $24$--$26$\,kEUR, which shapes the solution away from
physically implausible rapid cycling but corresponds to no operator cash flow. Together
these reproduce the residual to within about $1.5$\,kEUR per run, under one percent of the
objective.

The economic cost is reported because it is what an operator pays and the only quantity a
dispatch decision changes. Every bias and regret figure is computed \emph{directly} on this
economic cost, so neither accounting term enters a reported comparison: the loss/topology
decomposition (Table~\ref{tab:decomposition}) is formed from the four levels' economic costs and
closes with zero residual by construction, and the objective residual---together with its
roughly $5.6$\,kEUR variation across levels---sits entirely outside it. The residual is not
claimed to cancel; it never enters the comparison in the first place. The copperplate's
estimation bias reads $-11.8$\,\% on the raw objective and $\mathbf{-15.1}$\,\% on the
economic cost: the same finding, measured against a base that does or does not include
terms no operator sees.

The carbon convention is itself a modelling decision, stated explicitly: exported
cogeneration electricity is credited with the emissions it displaces at the grid margin.
The alternative gross convention shifts magnitudes by two to three percentage points
(the copperplate bias reads $-12.2$\,\% rather than $-15.1$\,\%) and changes neither the
sign of any bias or regret figure nor the loss-dominance result, because the credit is
near-identical at every level. The per-level split is released as
\texttt{objective\_decomposition.csv} so that either basis can be reconstructed.

One residual concern is that the schedules are optimised on the solver objective, so
aligning the objective to the reported economic cost could in principle shift the
decomposition. This is tested directly. The storage-cycling penalty is the only objective
term that both differs from the economic cost and is a well-posed optimisation term---the
carbon self-use credit is a reporting allocation that, placed in the objective, would reward
selling electricity purely to shed carbon, so it is excluded, and the gross-basis figures
above are the objective-consistent report for carbon. Removing the cycling penalty and
re-solving the four levels to the same $0.01$\,\% gap lowers every economic cost by a
near-uniform $3.5$\,\%: the loss share moves only from $95.8$ to $95.6$\,\% and topology
from $4.7$ to $5.0$\,\%. The decomposition is thus stable to the objective--economic
distinction, not merely arithmetically separated from it
(\texttt{loss\_share\_econobj\_sensitivity.csv}).
