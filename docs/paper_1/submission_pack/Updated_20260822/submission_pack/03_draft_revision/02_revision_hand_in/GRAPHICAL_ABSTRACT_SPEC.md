# Graphical abstract — what must change

Source: `Graphical_abstract_revision.pptx` in this folder. Two corrections were already
identified as needed, and the revision has since made a third necessary.

---

## 1. The headline number is wrong

The original abstract's figure was the L1→L3 gap of **13 %**. Under the new taxonomy that
comparison is CP→L1, and the number is **15.1 %** on economic cost (11.8 % on the solver
objective). The response letter lists a "percentage inconsistency between the graphical
abstract and the text" among the proactive disclosures — this is it, and it must be
corrected, not merely disclosed.

## 2. Asset placement

Already flagged: the generation assets are drawn at the wrong node. All generation in
Memmingen is co-located at the plant (j₁) — that is what makes it a purely
central-generation case, and it is load-bearing for the moderator argument in §3.5. A
graphic implying distributed assets contradicts a scope claim the paper makes repeatedly.

## 3. The message itself should change

This is the substantive one. The original graphic communicated *topology resolution matters*.
That is no longer the paper's finding. The revision's result is that **loss visibility, not
topology, sets the requirement** — and the title changed accordingly. A graphical abstract
still selling the old claim will be the first thing a reviewer sees and the fastest way to
suggest the revision is cosmetic.

### Suggested content

The strongest single image is the **bias-versus-regret contrast**, because it is the result
a reader remembers and it is visually self-explanatory:

- Two bars for the copperplate: **−15.1 % bias** (appears cheaper) and **+46.1 % regret**
  (costs more to run), pointing in opposite directions across the zero line.
- One annotation naming the mechanism: *blind to network loss → under-provisions the heat
  pump by 246 hours → pays at the margin in winter.*
- A small inset or side panel with the decomposition: **loss 95.8 % / topology 4.7 %**,
  making clear which of the two the fidelity requirement follows.

An alternative, if a single-panel process graphic is preferred, is the λ nomogram
(`F_rule`): loss number in, screening decision out. It is the most actionable result and
travels well as a standalone image — but it explains the *tool* rather than the *finding*,
so it is the second choice.

### What to avoid

- Any phrasing built on "dominates" or "accuracy". Both are what R1.1 and R1.2 objected to,
  and both are absent from the manuscript now.
- The five-level ladder as the organising visual. R2.1's objection was precisely that the
  paper read as a five-model comparison; a graphic organised that way reinstates the
  impression the text works to remove.
